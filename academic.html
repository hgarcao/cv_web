Overview

Research profile

My research sits at the intersection of physical oceanography and environmental engineering: how numerical models can be used to understand and predict the behaviour of marine, coastal and fluvial systems under natural and anthropogenic forcing. A thread from my MSc onwards is the Lagrangian framework — particle-tracking methods that follow pollutants and floating objects through ocean currents — and its stochastic use to quantify risk.

At the MSc level I developed a Lagrangian oil spill model with wind drift and slick spreading. That work led to oil spill and dispersion modelling for environmental licensing, to a Fortran drift model that became the SPAD-SAR search-and-rescue R&D programme, and to the Costa Norte project, where I was technical coordinator of a multi-institution programme on the vulnerability of Amazon Equatorial Margin mangroves to offshore oil spills.

My current PhD research develops a methodological framework for probabilistic flood risk estimation in data-scarce river basins, with application to territorial planning. The work integrates hydrological modelling, hydraulic modelling and stochastic sampling of input variables, propagating uncertainty throughout the modelling chain to produce probabilistic flood inundation maps. Though distinct in application from my earlier coastal and marine work, the underlying logic is the same: quantifying environmental risk under conditions of incomplete information.
