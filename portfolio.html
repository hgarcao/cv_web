Demo 1 · Hydrodynamics

Coastal Currents — Vitória Bay

Currents in an estuary and on the adjacent shelf set how pollutants, sediments and floating objects are carried, and they change with the tide and the wind. This viewer lets a reader step through a modelled 72-hour period and see the flow field, the wind and the water level together, instead of reading static maps.

The model is an unstructured-mesh hydrodynamic model run with Delft3D. The mesh is refined inside the estuary and coarser offshore, so the same viewer follows narrow channels and open shelf flow. The viewer animates the currents as particles or arrows and plots speed, direction and water level at any clicked point.

The project also covers the data side of numerical modelling: reducing a 395 MB model output file to about 85 kB per time step, building a land/water mask, and turning raw mesh values into something a browser can animate smoothly.

At a glance
ModelDelft3D, 2D (depth-averaged output)
Domainabout 20 km × 23 km (mesh area about 220 km²)
Mesh35,864 cells; nearest-neighbour spacing median 27 m, up to about 670 m offshore
Period10–13 Jun 2026 (UTC), output every 20 min
Variablescurrent components, water level, water depth

Press play to animate the 72-hour window; click the map for a time series; click the marker for the virtual tide station. Open full screen for the best experience.

Demo 2 · Waves

Coastal Waves — Arabian Gulf

Significant wave height, peak period and mean wave direction from a SWAN run over the Arabian Gulf, from the head of the Gulf to the Strait of Hormuz and the northern Gulf of Oman. The window starts with low waves from the south-east and ends with waves from the north-west that raise Hs in the central Gulf from about 0.3 m to above 0.8 m (maximum Hs in the window about 1.9 m).

The model was a personal development project; its results were never used operationally. The viewer colours the sea by Hs or Tp, animates particles that drift in the direction the waves travel at uniform speed, and plots Hs, Tp and direction for any clicked point.

At a glance
ModelSWAN, regular grid
Grid0.1° (97 × 67 points), 47.6–57.2° E, 23.8–30.4° N
Period26–31 Aug 2024 (UTC), hourly, 121 outputs
VariablesHs, peak period, mean and peak direction
Directionnautical (coming from); particles show propagation

Press play, switch between Hs and Tp, and click the sea for a time series. Open full screen for the best experience.
